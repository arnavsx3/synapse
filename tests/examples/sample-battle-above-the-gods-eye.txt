The Battle Above the Gods Eye

Spoiler summary inspired by George R. R. Martin's Fire & Blood

The Battle Above the Gods Eye was one of the most famous and terrible duels of the Dance of the Dragons. It was fought above the Gods Eye, the great lake beside Harrenhal, between Prince Daemon Targaryen and his nephew, Prince Aemond Targaryen.

Aemond rode Vhagar, the oldest and most powerful living dragon. Daemon rode Caraxes, a smaller but faster and more aggressive dragon. The two riders had been enemies throughout the civil war, and their conflict had become deeply personal after Aemond killed Lucerys Velaryon, Daemon's stepson, above Shipbreaker Bay.

When Aemond took control of Harrenhal, Daemon challenged him rather than retreating. For several days, Daemon waited near the lake, knowing that Aemond would eventually come to face him. The battle began when Vhagar and Caraxes met in the sky above the Gods Eye.

The dragons fought with enormous violence. Vhagar had greater size and strength, while Caraxes was quicker and more agile. They clawed, bit, and tore at one another as they fell through the clouds. Their riders continued the fight even as both dragons suffered terrible wounds.

At the decisive moment, Daemon leapt from Caraxes onto Vhagar's back. He crossed the distance between the dragons and drove his sword, Dark Sister, through Aemond's eye and into his skull. The blow killed Aemond, but it did not end the battle immediately.

Vhagar and Caraxes became locked together and plunged into the Gods Eye. Vhagar died in the lake, carrying Aemond's body with her. Caraxes survived the fall long enough to crawl onto the shore, where the wounded dragon died soon afterward.

Daemon's body was never found. Some believed he had died in the fall, while others claimed that he survived and disappeared. The uncertainty helped turn the battle into legend.

The duel changed the course of the Dance of the Dragons. Aemond, one of the war's most dangerous commanders, was dead, and the Greens had lost Vhagar, their greatest weapon. Daemon also vanished, leaving both sides weakened. The battle became remembered not as a victory for either faction, but as the final destruction of two legendary riders and their dragons.
